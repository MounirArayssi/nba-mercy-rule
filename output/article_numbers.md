# Article numbers: NBA Mercy Rule

Source: full run, 2015-16 to 2025-26 regular seasons, 13,202 games
(7 games excluded because of corrupt NBA play-by-play scores).
Rule: 40+ with 8:30 left, 30+ with 4:30, 25+ with 3:30, 20+ with 3:00 (Q4 only).

## Headline numbers

- **3,424 of 13,202 games (25.9%)** would have ended early.
- **Zero** of them would have changed the winner. No team ever came back.
- The closest any trailing team got: down 20 with 2:49 left, lost by 6
  (Pelicans at Bulls, Feb 6, 2020). Only **7** cut games finished within 10 points.
- **48,725 possessions** and about **11,000 minutes** of play removed,
  about 3.2 minutes per cut game.
- Average lead when the rule triggered: 25.6 points (median 23).
- Triggers by tier: conservative 1,878 · moderate 709 · aggressive 612 · extreme 225.

## Who gains and who loses

Pattern: **good teams get better, bad teams get worse.** The correlation between a
team's actual net rating and its mercy-rule change is 0.73.
- Top-5 teams: average +0.43 net, and 84% improve.
- Bottom-5 teams: average −0.50 net, and 93% get worse.
- Why: good teams' net ratings get dragged down when their benches play out
  blowouts. Bad teams' defense looks better than it is against opposing benches
  (most of their drop is DRtg rising).

## Rankings barely move

- **The #1 team never changes.** In all 11 seasons, the best net-rating team stays #1.
- The largest move in any season is 4 spots: 2019-20 Grizzlies, 16th → 20th.
  Every other season's largest move is 1–2 spots.
- Biggest climbers: 2019-20 Magic (19th → 16th), 2020-21 Nuggets (6th → 4th),
  2024-25 Knicks (8th → 6th).
- Takeaway: garbage time adds noise to net rating but doesn't distort it.

recent teams and raw defense lists are all 2015-16 teams. **Use the

## Standout games

**Largest lead when the rule triggered**
- **64 points**: Grizzlies vs. Thunder, Dec 2, 2021. Up 129–65 with 8:27 left,
  final 152–79 (the biggest margin of victory in NBA history, 73).
- 60: Raptors vs. Warriors, Apr 2, 2021 (119–59 with 8:27 left).
- 59: Hornets vs. Grizzlies, Mar 22, 2018 (120–61 with 8:20 left).
- 58: Pacers at Thunder, May 1, 2021 (133–75); Clippers vs. Nets, Jan 15, 2025 (111–53).

**Earliest possible trigger (8:30 left, extreme tier)**
- Trail Blazers at Suns, Oct 18, 2017 (opening week): up 51, 110–58.
- Others: Warriors at Jazz (Apr 10, 2018), Warriors at Thunder (Oct 27, 2019),
  Bucks at Heat (Dec 29, 2020), Warriors at Thunder (Apr 14, 2021).

**Closest finishes after a trigger (garbage time makes blowouts look closer)**
- Pelicans at Bulls, Feb 6, 2020: down 20 at 2:49, final 125–119 (a 6-point loss).
- Hornets at Bucks, Apr 9, 2021: Bucks down 20 at 2:35, lost by 8.
- Jazz at Trail Blazers, Dec 14, 2023: Blazers down 21 at 2:57, lost by 8.
- Lakers at Mavericks, Christmas 2022: Lakers down 21 at 3:00, lost by 9.

## Methodology lines

- 13,202 of 13,209 regular-season games; 7 excluded because the NBA's own
  play-by-play has corrupt score rows.
- Games stop at the end of the possession in progress when a rule triggers.
- Possessions are counted from play-by-play and are usually within 1 of NBA.com's count.
- 168 small box-score mismatches (almost all offensive rebounds off by 1).
  None affect points.
- This shortens real games. It doesn't model how teams would play if the rule existed.
